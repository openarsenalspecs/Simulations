# EarthRoot Network

EarthRoot Network is an open-source, AGPL 3.0+ licensed AI-assisted infrastructure design system that generates location-aware, modular renewable energy and material systems.

It is designed as a global design intelligence layer that helps users create optimized infrastructure based on local climate conditions, available materials, cost constraints, and ecological impact.

The system focuses on **modular design, regional adaptability, and long-term lifecycle efficiency (10–20 year target systems)**.

---

# Core Concept

EarthRoot Network treats every location on Earth as a unique design environment.

Instead of one-size-fits-all infrastructure, it generates:

- Region-specific energy systems  
- Locally optimized material sourcing plans  
- Modular construction blueprints  
- Hybrid renewable energy configurations  
- Lifecycle-aware maintenance systems  

> Energy systems shaped by place.

---

# Key Features

## 1. Location-Aware Design Engine

- Accepts GPS coordinates or region input
- Builds environmental profiles including:
  - wind behavior and turbulence
  - solar exposure patterns
  - temperature and humidity cycles
  - urban density and obstruction mapping
  - terrain and ecological conditions

Outputs:
- Energy potential scoring
- Infrastructure suitability analysis
- Environmental constraint mapping

---

## 2. Modular Infrastructure System

EarthRoot Network designs infrastructure as interchangeable modules.

Supported module types:
- Wind capture roofline systems
- Micro-turbine energy cartridges
- Passive airflow acceleration channels
- Structural reinforcement modules
- Environmental regulation systems (cooling, ventilation, moisture control)

All modules are:
- Swappable
- Regionally adaptable
- Repairable instead of fully replaced
- Designed for 10–20 year lifecycle cycles

---

## 3. Material Intelligence & Local Sourcing Engine

- Identifies regionally available construction materials
- Compares local sourcing vs import costs
- Optimizes for:
  - cost efficiency
  - carbon footprint
  - durability
  - transport impact

Material categories include:
- timber and engineered wood
- bamboo (climate dependent)
- hemp and flax fibers
- mycelium-based composites
- recycled industrial materials
- minimal synthetic electrical components

---

## 4. Wind Modeling & Roof Geometry Optimization

- Simulates airflow behavior across structures
- Applies Venturi acceleration principles
- Optimizes:
  - roof shape
  - intake placement
  - airflow channels
  - module density distribution

Outputs:
- parametric roof design models
- airflow efficiency maps
- predicted energy yield ranges

---

## 5. Energy Hybridization Layer

Supports multi-source renewable energy systems within a single design.

Energy sources include:
- wind energy systems
- solar photovoltaic integration
- thermal gradient energy capture
- rainwater kinetic energy systems
- passive airflow recovery systems

Features:
- dynamic energy balancing
- seasonal adaptation logic
- redundancy across energy sources
- compatibility enforcement across modules

Constraint:
- No single energy source may exceed 80% dependency in variable climates

---

## 6. Lifecycle Prediction Engine

- Models degradation over 10–20 year design horizon
- Predicts maintenance cycles
- Estimates structural fatigue and failure probability
- Generates replacement schedules

Outputs:
- lifecycle maps
- maintenance timelines
- material fatigue forecasts

---

## 7. Cost Optimization Engine

Multi-variable optimization system balancing:

- installation cost
- material sourcing cost
- labor cost (regional)
- transport emissions
- maintenance frequency
- energy output efficiency

Outputs:
- cost per kWh estimates
- total lifecycle cost projections
- optimized design tradeoff reports

---

## 8. Sensor-to-Model Feedback System

EarthRoot Network improves through real-world performance data.

Monitored signals:
- wind speed and turbulence
- structural vibration
- moisture and material degradation
- energy output per module
- thermal variation
- system failures and anomalies

System functions:
- normalized data formatting
- time-series performance tracking
- regional model adaptation
- optional global anonymized learning dataset

Outcome:
> Continuous improvement of design accuracy and material selection.

---

## 9. Failure Learning Engine

- Classifies real-world failures:
  - material degradation
  - structural fatigue
  - environmental mismatch
  - design inefficiency
  - installation variance

- Converts failures into design improvements:
  - updated geometry recommendations
  - better material substitutions
  - improved lifecycle modeling

---

## 10. Regional Adaptive AI Models

- Each region operates a localized AI model instance
- Learns from local environmental and performance data
- Adjusts design outputs independently per region

No centralized override of regional learning.

---

## 11. Global Design Intelligence Layer

- Provides baseline global design knowledge
- Aggregates climate and performance data
- Enables cross-region learning without forcing uniform design

---

## 12. Continuous Optimization Loop

The system continuously improves through:

1. Data collection from deployed systems  
2. Comparison of predicted vs real performance  
3. Detection of inefficiencies or failures  
4. Model parameter updates  
5. Updated design generation outputs  

---

## 13. Material Ecology & Growth Zone Mapping

- Identifies optimal regions for material production:
  - hemp cultivation zones
  - fast-growth timber regions
  - bamboo-compatible climates
  - mycelium production hubs

- Compares:
  - local cultivation cost
  - import cost + emissions
  - yield reliability

Outputs:
- regional material sourcing maps
- bio-based supply chain optimization plans

---

## 14. Deployment Generator

Converts AI designs into build-ready outputs:

- modular construction blueprints
- material procurement lists
- installation sequencing plans
- maintenance schedules
- lifecycle projections

---

## 15. Design Versioning System

Infrastructure designs are treated like software:

- version-controlled blueprints (v1.0, v1.1, etc.)
- diff-based improvements between versions
- regional forks of designs
- rollback capability for failed deployments

---

## 16. Design Provenance Layer

Every generated system includes:

- AI model version used
- dataset snapshot reference
- material assumptions
- confidence scores per subsystem
- environmental constraint inputs

---

## 17. Real-World Constraint Engine

Ensures all designs are buildable:

- zoning compliance modeling
- building code constraints
- flood/fire risk overlays
- insurance viability constraints

---

## 18. Community Validation Layer

- Users and builders can report real-world outcomes
- Captures:
  - system failures
  - performance deviations
  - improvement suggestions
- Feeds directly into AI training loop

---

## 19. Sensor Network Standardization

Defines universal telemetry formats for:

- environmental readings
- structural performance data
- energy output tracking
- degradation monitoring

Ensures compatibility across all deployments.

---

## 20. Open Knowledge Graph

A relational infrastructure intelligence system connecting:

- materials ↔ climates
- modules ↔ performance outcomes
- failures ↔ environmental conditions
- costs ↔ geography

Enables cross-region learning without central control.

---

## 21. Module Interchange Standard

Defines universal interface rules for physical modules:

- mechanical mounting standards
- electrical interface compatibility
- airflow performance classes
- serviceability and replacement rules

Enables interoperability across all EarthRoot deployments.

---

- Location-optimized renewable energy systems

- Local material sourcing strategies
